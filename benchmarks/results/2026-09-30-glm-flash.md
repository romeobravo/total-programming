# GLM 5.3 Flash — pi harness (2026-09-30)

Third arm benchmark on a much stronger model tier than the Haiku runs:
GLM 5.3 Flash served through Z.ai's coding-plan endpoint, executed by the
pi coding agent harness (`--runner pi`, `--provider zai-coding`) instead of
the Claude Code CLI. 228 cells = 19 tasks × 3 arms (baseline,
total-programming, ponytail) × 4 repetitions. Attempt 1 ran a 300 s
per-cell cap; the 54 cells that timed out were re-run once at 600 s and
the 30 still missing once at 1200 s, all pooled into this dataset. Per
project owner decision (2026-09-29), timeout rates are **not** analysed —
no fair cap is knowable; timed-out cells simply contribute no deliverable.
19 of 228 cells remain undelivered at 1200 s (baseline 10, Total
Programming 9, ponytail 0).

Harness notes: the pi runner caps a cell's `_pi_events.jsonl` at 256 MB
(GLM's long thinking streamed per-delta snapshots grew single files to
600 MB and OOM-killed the host twice before the guard). `--thinking` is
not set, so the provider default (thinking enabled) applies.

## Delivery (valid runs at the 1200 s cap)

| arm | delivered | correct | safe |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 66/76 | 65/66 | 66/66 |
| total-programming | 67/76 | 67/67 | 67/67 |
| ponytail | 76/76 | 76/76 | 76/76 |

Correctness and safety on delivered runs are at ceiling for every arm.

## Delivered LOC (paired per task, median per repetition set, all 19 tasks)

| arm | geometric mean vs baseline |
|---|---|
| total-programming | 76% |
| ponytail | 44% |

Contrast with the Haiku runs, where Total Programming cut 43%: the much
stronger baseline arrives lean already, and the paired cut shrinks to
~24%. Total Programming's largest relative cuts are on the mid-size
features (csv-sum 40%, critic-email 55%, dropzone 54%, datepicker 63%);
it delivers MORE than baseline on archive (145%) and rate-limit (105%).

## Cost and time (median per valid run)

| arm | tokens in+out | wall time |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 19.1 k | 154 s |
| total-programming | 23.2 k | 161 s |
| ponytail | 12.5 k | 92 s |

GLM list pricing was unavailable on the coding plan, so no $/run. Total
Programming spends ~20% more tokens than baseline — the judgment overhead
is real at this tier — while ponytail is the cheapest and fastest.

## Cyclomatic complexity (lizard, all 12 feature + 7 safety tasks, full cohort)

Mean of per-task medians per function:

| arm | feature: n_fn | feature: cc mean / max | safety: cc mean / max |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 16.8 | 2.73 / 6.6 | 5.11 / 5.9 |
| total-programming | 13.5 | 2.65 / 5.3 | 3.52 / 4.1 |
| ponytail | 7.5 | 2.23 / 3.5 | 2.25 / 2.6 |

The Haiku pattern replicates, weaker: per-function complexity ≈ baseline
on features and ~31% lower on the safety tasks. Ponytail is structurally
simplest everywhere (no function above CC 3.5).

## Blind readability judge (Sonnet, anonymous A/B, position-balanced)

Pairs score both solutions 1–5 and pick a preference; only pairs with
source on both sides are counted.

- **Total Programming vs baseline: 11–7 for Total Programming** (19 pairs
  with code, 1 tie; 5 excluded pairs where a side delivered nothing).
  Mean scores 3.16 vs 2.89. On Haiku this was 16–8 — the edge survives at
  a weaker magnitude.
- **Ponytail vs Total Programming: 19–3 for ponytail** (24 pairs, 2
  ties). Mean scores 4.00 vs 2.46. On Haiku this was 14–7 — ponytail's
  readability advantage grows with model capability.

## Reading

On a much stronger model, principle steering still pays but buys less:
Total Programming keeps a modest LOC cut (−24%), a small blind-readability
edge (11–7), and its signature safety-task simplification (−31% CC) — at
~20% more tokens and similar wall time. The mechanical minimalism of
ponytail dominates this tier: complete delivery, −56% LOC, the simplest
structure, fastest and cheapest, and a decisive blind head-to-head win.
Neither arm loses correctness. What shrinks with capability is the
baseline's over-engineering — the problem both skills exist to solve —
and with it the measurable value of injecting any counterweight.

Caveats: single run, single model, one judge model (Sonnet); the Haiku
runs used the Claude Code harness so Haiku-vs-GLM differences conflate
model tier with harness; ponytail's skill text was appended raw including
its YAML frontmatter (same method as the Haiku run, so arms are comparable
within the run); timeout caps are arbitrary and excluded from analysis.
